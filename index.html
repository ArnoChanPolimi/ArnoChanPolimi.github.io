CV: I am a master's student in Telecommunication Engineering at Politecnico di Milano, doing my thesis in the BONSAI Lab (DEIB) with Qiaolun Zhang and Massimo Tornatore.

My thesis is about AI training that spans several datacenters. When GPUs in different sites train one model, their collective communication has to cross a wide-area network whose free capacity keeps changing. I built a four-GPU testbed to see what happens when the collective schedule and the WAN paths are adapted together, and wrote the runtime code that stops the two from getting in each other's way.

From September 2025 to January 2026 I was an Erasmus+ exchange student at ENSEA in Cergy, France, taking courses in networks, wireless communications and security.

I did my B.Sc. in Electronic Information Engineering at Beijing Institute of Technology, where my undergraduate thesis, supervised by Prof. Qin Zhang, simulated synchronization and distributed beamforming in wireless ad hoc networks. After graduating, I worked in industry as a software engineer on carrier-grade network software, mainly packet forwarding and IPsec in C, before coming back to study for my master's.

Research

Communication Systems for Distributed AI Training

The testbed runs real PyTorch FSDP training on four NVIDIA P100 GPUs, split into two emulated datacenters. Between them is a multipath WAN built with Mininet and Open vSwitch, where I set the bandwidth, delay and background traffic of every path. The GPUs talk through NCCL/MSCCL over RoCEv2 RDMA.

Two controls run during training. The fast one moves single collective channels to a less loaded WAN path; for this I patched NCCL so that each channel uses its own RoCEv2 source port, which the routers can match. The slow one generates a new All-Gather schedule and switches all ranks to it at the same training step. When both run, a path change during a schedule switch can stall the collective, so an interlock holds path changes back until the switch is done.

In the workshop paper, using both controls cut All-Gather latency by 34.7% against a fixed baseline. In the thesis I extended the work to Reduce-Scatter and to the full training step: giving the slow links fewer Reduce-Scatter chunks halved its time, and under replayed capacity limits the training step became about 20% shorter.

Selected Projects

BER-Based O-RAN Closed-Loop Control
Extended a gNB emulator and its Protobuf messages so that it reports each user's bit error rate (BER) and modulation and coding scheme (MCS), and wrote a Python xApp that sets the MCS of each user over E2. Tested with four emulated users and a 500 ms monitoring interval. The BER values are synthetic, so this checks the control loop, not real link performance.
